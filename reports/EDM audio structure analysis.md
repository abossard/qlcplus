# Offline analysis can map EDM tracks

Offline analysis of an EDM library is now good enough to drive lighting for beats, downbeats, section boundaries, drops, buildups, vocal presence and an energy curve. It is not good enough for named synth roles or mood words. The decisive 2026 result is that in-domain EDM training changes everything: an All-In-One model trained on the 1,423-track Raveform EDM set reaches **beat F1 0.991, downbeat F1 0.965 and boundary HR.5F 0.835**, while the same architecture trained on pop (Harmonix) drops to **downbeat 0.753, HR.5F 0.635 and pairwise label F 0.542** on EDM ([Raveform, TISMIR](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)). The catch is that the EDM-trained weights are announced ("v2 will be released soon") but not public ([Raveform project page](https://mir-aidj.github.io/raveform/)), and pop-trained section labels are close to useless on EDM (SongFormer zero-shot label accuracy **0.148** on EDM-98) ([EDMFormer](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08759v1)). The practical answer for the fork is therefore a staged overhaul: a Python 3.13 batch tool that combines Beat This for beats and downbeats, RoFormer or Demucs stems for vocal, drum and bass activity, and an EDM rule layer on a 16-bar phrase grid for drops and buildups. It writes one versioned JSON per track that QLC+ reads with the nlohmann::json it already vendors. A small rating website measures where it fails. The live aubio path stays for unknown audio, the research branch's C++-embedded Essentia plus SQLite design should be dropped, and its data model kept. Expect roughly 1 to 2 minutes per track on the local M1 Max, so the 800-track library costs one or two overnight runs.

## EDM-trained models beat pop models by 0.2 in boundary accuracy

Every serious structure model in 2026 learned its sense of "section" from a dataset, and the dataset decides the vocabulary. The mature, pip-installable option is **allin1** (Kim and Nam, WASPAA 2023, MIT), which outputs BPM, beats, downbeats and labelled segments in one JSON. Its labels are pop ones (intro, verse, chorus, break, bridge, inst, solo, outro) with no buildup or drop ([allin1 GitHub](https://github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one)). On Harmonix it scores beat F1 .958, downbeat .915 and HR.5F .660, against .497 for the classic self-similarity approach still found in MSAF ([allin1 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.16425v1.pdf)). The strongest released pop model is **SongFormer** (Oct 2025, CC-BY-4.0), which fuses MuQ and MusicFM features and reaches HR.5F 0.703 and label accuracy 0.795 on SongFormBench-Harmonix. Its training data does not mention EDM ([SongFormer paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02797v2)).

Two 2026 projects retrained these architectures on EDM vocabularies. **Raveform** (TISMIR 2026) annotated 1,423 tracks with beats, downbeats and energy-defined labels (intro, buildup, breakdown, drop, cooldown, bridge, outro, ambient-intro, ambient-outro) and is the only peer-reviewed, cross-validated EDM benchmark ([Raveform, TISMIR](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)). **EDMFormer** (March 2026 preprint) retrained SongFormer on 98 tracks and lifted label accuracy from 0.148 to 0.883, but its test set is 10 tracks, the paper contradicts itself on annotator count, and neither code nor weights are announced ([EDMFormer](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08759v1)). The pattern in both is the same: **boundaries transfer moderately from pop (HR.5F about 0.57 to 0.64), labels do not**. EDMFormer's authors explain why: EDM sections are defined by brightness, rhythmic density and low-frequency energy, not by the harmonic and lyric repetition pop models key on.

| System | Trained on | Tested on | Beat F1 | Downbeat F1 | HR.5F | Label score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All-In-One (Raveform) | Raveform EDM | Raveform, 8-fold CV | 0.991 | 0.965 | 0.835 | PWF 0.847 |
| All-In-One (Harmonix) | Harmonix pop | Raveform | 0.930 | 0.753 | 0.635 | PWF 0.542 |
| madmom | generic | Raveform | 0.947 | 0.669 | n/a | n/a |
| EDMFormer | EDM-98 | 10 EDM tracks | n/a | n/a | 0.616 | ACC 0.883 |
| SongFormer zero-shot | pop | 10 EDM tracks | n/a | n/a | 0.569 | ACC 0.148 |
| SongFormer | Harmonix | Harmonix | n/a | n/a | 0.703 | ACC 0.795 |

Sources: [Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288), [EDMFormer](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08759v1), [SongFormer](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02797v2).

The Raveform HR.5F of 0.835, higher than any pop result, says EDM is the easier genre once a model is trained in-domain. That fits the regularity documented by Zehren et al.: switch points always sit on a downbeat at the start of a period ([Zehren et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.08411)), and DJ cue points cluster at 16-bar, then 8-bar, multiples ([CUE-DETR](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06823v1)). This regularity is the main lever for a tool without EDM weights. A pop model, or plain novelty curves, proposes approximate boundaries. Snapping them to a downbeat-anchored 16-bar grid and labelling each phrase from stem energy should recover much of the gap. That last step is an inference, not a measured result, and is exactly what the rating site has to confirm.

### Drops and buildups are energy events on a phrase grid

No public detector for risers, snare rolls or filter sweeps comes with benchmark numbers. What does exist agrees on the cues. Yadati et al. (ISMIR 2014) define a drop as "where the buildup ends and the bassline is re-introduced". Their segmentation-plus-classifier pipeline puts candidates a mean 2.5 s from the true drop, misses under 8%, and reaches F1 0.71 at a 15 s tolerance and above 0.6 at 3 s ([Yadati et al.](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000297.pdf)). A 2026 hobby project with 29 context features (bass ratio, 4/8/16/32-bar alignment, energy versus the preceding buildup) and XGBoost claims F1 0.95, but states neither dataset size nor tolerance ([drop-detector](https://github.com/felixmeyer6/drop-detector)), so treat it as unverified. Learned EDM-taxonomy models subsume these heuristics once weights exist. Until then, the defensible rule layer is the one these sources imply. Mark a drop at the first downbeat where low-band and kick energy jump back after a phrase with little low end and rising brightness or onset density. Mark a buildup as that preceding phrase, and a breakdown where kick density stays low for 8 bars or more. Demucs or RoFormer drum and bass stems give cleaner kick and bass curves than filtering the full mix.

## Stems make vocals reliable but leave synths in one bucket

Source separation is the most mature and Mac-ready part of the stack. On MVSEP's Multisong benchmark the BS-RoFormer family leads: the best single vocal model scores **12.33 dB** SDR against 8.33 for htdemucs_ft, and the best 2025 ensemble scores 11.93 / 14.85 / 14.33 / 9.00 dB on vocals, bass, drums and other, against 8.33 / 12.05 / 11.24 / 5.74 for htdemucs_ft ([MVSEP algorithms](https://mvsep.com/en/algorithms)). A Mel-RoFormer DrumSep model separates kick (22.22 dB) and snare (17.09 dB), which gives separate kick and snare activity tracks for strobe and chase cues. A karaoke model splits lead (10.77 dB) from backing vocals (7.03 dB). Demucs itself has been archived since January 2025, and adefossez/demucs is now the maintained fork ([facebookresearch/demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs); [adefossez/demucs](https://github.com/adefossez/demucs)). On Apple Silicon, `audio-separator` runs RoFormer checkpoints on MPS ([audio-separator](https://pypi.org/project/audio-separator/)), and its MLX port measured 2.16 to 2.50 times faster on an M4 mini ([mlx-audio-separator](https://pypi.org/project/mlx-audio-separator/0.1.16/)).

Vocal presence is effectively solved by "separate first, then threshold". Plain RMS thresholding of Demucs-separated vocals reaches **F1 0.949** on Jamendo, within about one point of trained Transformer detectors (0.960), while RMS on the raw mix scores 0.679 ([Bonzi et al., MLSP 2023](https://michelemancusi.netlify.app/publication/svd/svd.pdf)). The same method drops to F1 0.793 on MedleyDB with precision 0.688, because bleed causes false positives. That argues for per-track, percentile-based thresholds with hysteresis rather than one absolute level, and RoFormer's 3 to 4 dB cleaner vocals should help, though that is unmeasured. Telling a lead vocal from a chop has no published tool. A heuristic built from segment duration, rhythmic quantisation, the karaoke lead/back split and an optional lyric-transcription check is plausible but untested. Heavily vocoded or pitched EDM vocals may partly land in the "other" stem.

Instruments are where the evidence thins. The "other" stem, about 9 dB SDR even for the best ensemble, holds leads, pads, plucks, risers and FX together, so its RMS answers "something melodic is playing", not what. No tagger has been benchmarked on EDM sound-design classes. AudioSet taggers give general frame classes (synthesizer, drums, singing) with documented mAP: PANNs CNN14 0.431, BEATs and EAT about 0.48 ([PANNs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.10211); [arXiv 2601.21612](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21612)). Zero-shot CLAP text prompts are unreliable for instruments. An ISMIR 2024 study found the text branch is the weak link, with similarity distributions that overlap heavily, and found that audio-derived label embeddings roughly double the metrics ([arXiv 2407.18058](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18058v1)). The supported route to "pad versus lead versus riser" is therefore a few-shot linear probe on frozen MERT, MuQ or CLAP audio embeddings trained on a few hundred hand-labelled clips from the user's own library. MERT weights are CC-BY-NC-4.0 ([MERT-v1-95M](https://huggingface.co/m-a-p/MERT-v1-95M)), and CLAP via `laion/larger_clap_music` is Apache-2.0 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/laion/larger_clap_music)).

### Arousal tracks energy, while atmosphere words remain unvalidated

Arousal can be followed over time with acceptable reliability. Valence is consistently harder ([DSAML, arXiv 2412.19200](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19200v1)). The only large dynamic-annotation dataset is still DEAM, with 1,802 songs at 2 Hz ([DEAM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5345802)). Essentia's arousal/valence regressors and binary mood heads (aggressive, happy, party, relaxed, sad) can run on sliding windows, but they were trained on clip-level, mostly non-EDM data, and the models are **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** ([Essentia models](https://essentia.upf.edu/models.html)). In EDM, within-track arousal largely tracks drums, bass and density, so a stem-derived energy curve is likely as useful for lighting as learned arousal, and it is free of licence problems. Raveform's label set doubles as a coarse tension curve: cooldown and the ambient intro and outro already encode low-energy atmosphere ([Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)). For descriptive words per section, music LLMs improved sharply. Music Flamingo (NVIDIA, ICLR 2026) scores 8.8 against 7.2 for Qwen3-Omni on MusicCaps ([Music Flamingo](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/adlr/MF)). Zero-shot audio LLMs still have poor timestamp precision ([TAC, arXiv 2602.15766](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15766)), so they fit only as an optional per-section captioner, never as the timeline source. The Music Flamingo licence and Apple Silicon support were not verified.

## Raveform and the user's own library are the only usable EDM test sets

General structure datasets are pop-centric. Harmonix's 129 Dance/Electronic tracks contain **4 "build" and 0 "drop" labels**, which makes them useless as drop ground truth without relabelling, though they remain fine for beat and downbeat timing on dance-pop ([harmonixset segments](https://github.com/urinieto/harmonixset/tree/main/dataset/segments)). The candidate sets compare as follows.

No public EDM vocal-activity, EDM instrument-activity or EDM mood dataset exists, and no EDM structure set has independent double annotations, so there is no published human ceiling for EDM. Two free shortcuts exist. Matching the 800 MP3s by artist, title and duration against Raveform's `tracks.jsonl` and EDM-CUE yields external annotations for owned files. And the VirtualDJ `database.xml` that QLC+ already reads (`qmlui/vdjdatabasereader.h`) holds user-curated beat grids and cue points, which serve as beat-grid ground truth and a fallback.

Evaluation should use `mir_eval`: `segment.detection` at 0.5 s and 3 s windows, `segment.pairwise` and `nce`, and `beat.f_measure` (±70 ms) plus `beat.continuity` on beats and on downbeats, as Raveform does ([mir_eval segment](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/segment.html); [mir_eval beat](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/beat.html)). For drops, beat-relative windows fit EDM better than Yadati's 3 to 15 s windows. CUE-DETR evaluates at one beat (~344 ms) ([CUE-DETR](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06823v1)). Report drop-onset F1 at half a beat, one bar and four bars, plus drop-count error and main-drop time error, because a light show lives or dies on the main drop. Nieto et al. recommend hit rate over median deviation and note that listeners prefer fewer correct boundaries to more imprecise ones ([TISMIR 2020 review](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.54)).

## Recommended pipeline replaces offline guesswork, not the live engine

### What the fork has today and what survives

Everything in `engine/audio/src` is a live, streaming analyser. `AubioProcessor` computes onsets, tempo, MFCC, mel banks and spectral features, but counts `beatInBar` with a fixed four beats per bar rather than detecting downbeats. `BeatTracker` is compiled into the library but referenced only by its tests and bench. Nothing in `qmlui/` stores sections or drops. The only per-song metadata used today is external: the VDJ grid and cue POIs, and the ID3 TBPM tag (`AudioBpmTag`). The research branch `origin/claude/research-audio-analysis-N5WGL` is mostly a 697-line plan (`docs/audio-dsp-upgrade-plan.md`) for C++-linked Essentia (AGPL), a SQLite store, Olaf fingerprinting and a tiered position source. Its own milestone snapshot marks M0 missing, M1 partial and M2 to M5 missing, and its live scaffold is older than the richer aubio code now on `mcp-server`.

The overhaul is worth doing because none of the existing code can produce downbeats, phrases, drops or vocal regions, and the research plan's own accuracy target (drops ±1 s on 5 tracks) is far below what 2026 tools allow. Keep the live aubio path (`AubioProcessor`, `AudioChannel`/`AudioSnapshot`, `MelPostProcessor`) for unknown or live audio. Reuse the research branch's data model (sha256 track key, beats with bar index, `structural_events` kinds build/drop/break/outro, `AudioFeatures.source = Cached`). Drop C++-embedded Essentia and SQLite-first storage, because Python per-track JSON is easier to review, diff and rebuild. Olaf and position tracking are a separate live-sync problem.

### The batch pipeline

Each stage writes its own cached result keyed by file sha256, stage, tool version and model id, so the merged per-track file can be rebuilt cheaply whenever heuristics change.

| Stage | Tool | Output | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Decode | ffmpeg to 44.1 kHz WAV once | PCM cache | avoids MP3 decoder offsets of 20 to 40 ms ([allin1](https://github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one)) |
| 2. Beats and downbeats | Beat This `final0` (MIT, `pip install beat-this`) | beat and downbeat times | Harmonix beat 95.8 / downbeat 90.7 ([Beat This](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21658v1)); 4:32 track in 5.1 s on M4 CPU ([beat-this crate](https://docs.rs/crate/beat-this/0.3.0/source/README.md)) |
| 3. Grid fit | own code | constant (or piecewise) tempo grid, downbeat phase by track-wide vote, 16-bar phrase anchor | corrects the weak point (generic downbeats 0.67 to 0.75 on EDM) |
| 4. Separation | demucs-mlx htdemucs first; audio-separator BS-RoFormer vocals plus DrumSep as upgrade | vocals, drums, bass, other, kick, snare | htdemucs 120 s in 1.05 s with MLX on M4 Max ([demucs-mlx](https://pypi.org/project/demucs-mlx/)) |
| 5. Activity and energy curves | librosa on stems and mix | 10 to 20 Hz curves: stem activity 0..1, low/mid/high, RMS, onset density, centroid | per-track percentile thresholds with hysteresis |
| 6. Boundaries | allin1 from PR #39 branch (optional, isolated env), plus novelty on embeddings | candidate boundaries and pop labels as features | PR #39 removes NATTEN; structure model 11 s CPU on M5 for 2:24 ([PR #39](https://github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one/pull/39)) |
| 7. EDM labeller | own rule layer, later swapped for Raveform weights or a probe trained on Raveform | sections intro/build/drop/breakdown/outro, events drop/build_start | snap to downbeats, prefer 8/16/32-bar positions |
| 8. Tags (optional) | BEATs or PANNs on "other" stem; few-shot probe on MERT/CLAP embeddings | coarse instrument and role scores | probe needs a few hundred labelled clips |
| 9. Merge | own code | `<sha256>.analysis.json` | derived, rebuildable |

Runtime on the local M1 Max is unmeasured. Scaling from published Apple numbers gives roughly **60 to 120 s per 6-minute track, or 13 to 27 hours for 800 tracks**, as a resumable overnight batch with GPU-bound and CPU-bound stages in parallel processes. Four stems for 800 tracks are about 100 GB as FLAC, so keep only the activity curves, or 22 kHz mono stems. Measure one reference track per stage before committing.

### Output format QLC+ can consume directly

Use one versioned JSON per track in the spirit of JAMS (time, duration, value, confidence per observation) ([JAMS namespaces](https://jams.readthedocs.io/en/stable/namespace.html)), with DJ-style fields borrowed from rekordbox's PSSI phrase data, which stores phrase starts in beat numbers and a high/mid/low mood per phrase ([Deep Symmetry ANLZ](https://djl-analysis.deepsymmetry.org/rekordbox-export-analysis/anlz.html)).

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `schema_version`, `track` | path, sha256, duration_s, title, artist |
| `analyzers[]` | stage, tool, version, model, params_hash, created |
| `tempo`, `grid` | bpm, confidence, stable; `anchor_s` and `period_s` when constant (same shape as VDJ Scan/Phase) |
| `beats[]` | t, bar, beat_in_bar (1 = downbeat), confidence |
| `sections[]` | start_s, end_s, start_bar, bars, label (intro, build, drop, breakdown, outro), energy, confidence, source |
| `events[]` | t, kind (drop, build_start, riser, impact, silence), bar, confidence, source |
| `stems` | hop_s, names, activity curves 0..1 (sidecar `.f32` when large) |
| `curves` | hop_s, rms_db, low, mid, high, onset_strength |
| `tags[]` | label, score, source |

On the C++ side this maps onto existing structures. `VdjDatabaseReader::SongGrid` (beat period, anchor, POIs) already feeds the Show editor's VDJBeat mode, `ShowFactory` already builds one Show per song, and Function beat speeds use 1000 = 1 beat. Sections become Show tracks or items, and the cached curves can replay through an `AudioFeatures.source = Cached` path. Human ratings live in a separate file so re-analysis never overwrites them.

### The rating website

Build a static page on wavesurfer.js v8 (BSD-3, released 2026-11-02, Regions and Spectrogram plugins, accepts precomputed `peaks` and `duration`) ([wavesurfer.js](https://github.com/katspaugh/wavesurfer.js); [options](https://wavesurfer.xyz/docs/types/wavesurfer.WaveSurferOptions)), served by a minimal local Python HTTP server that stores ratings in JSON or SQLite keyed by sha, stage, event id and analyser version. Python cuts a 16 s MP3 excerpt around each predicted event with ffmpeg and precomputes the peaks, so the browser never decodes full tracks. Each excerpt shows the waveform, the predicted section regions, energy and stem-activity lanes and a beat ruler, with a generated click track mixed in on beats and accented on downbeats so grid errors are audible. The questions stay small and fast. For beats: is the click on the beat, and is the accent on the "one"? For a boundary or drop: correct, off by N bars (early or late), wrong label, or not a boundary? Add a per-section label pick, a "missed event here" button, and a 1 to 5 "usable for lighting" score per track.

The protocol follows from the evidence. Drops alone took one annotator about 6 hours per 100 tracks ([Yadati et al.](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000297.pdf)). Full structure took three experts seven months for 1,423 tracks ([Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)). So target a **50 to 100 track test set** stratified by subgenre, BPM bin, vocal versus instrumental and hard cases (no-drop tracks, double drops, long ambient intros, extended versus radio edits), matching published EDM test sizes of 98 and 101. Annotate 10 to 20 tracks twice, weeks apart and from scratch, to get a personal human ceiling and to counter anchoring on the model's pre-filled boundaries. Freeze the test set, tune thresholds only on a separate dev split, and compare system versions with a paired per-track Wilcoxon test rather than means. Rating pre-filled output is much cheaper than annotating from scratch, but no source quantifies the anchoring bias it introduces, which is why the from-scratch subset matters.

**Stack and environment.** Use uv with a project-local **Python 3.13**. It is the newest version where torch 2.15, librosa 1.0, demucs, audio-separator's dependencies, onnxruntime and essentia(-tensorflow) dev1389 all ship macOS arm64 wheels. Python 3.15 (the machine's default `python3`) blocks demucs via sphn, audio-separator via onnxruntime and samplerate, and Essentia ([PyPI essentia](https://pypi.org/pypi/essentia/json); [PyPI sphn](https://pypi.org/pypi/sphn/json); [PyPI onnxruntime](https://pypi.org/pypi/onnxruntime/json)). Environment A (`analysis`) holds torch, torchaudio, beat-this, demucs or demucs-mlx, audio-separator, librosa, soundfile and transformers. Load CLAP through transformers, because `laion-clap` and `msclap` pin numpy<2, which has no cp313 wheels ([PyPI laion-clap](https://pypi.org/pypi/laion-clap/json)). Environment B (`allin1`, optional) holds allin1 from PR #39 plus madmom from git main or PR #559. Isolate it because upstream allin1 was last pushed in 2024 and NATTEN has no Mac wheels and a broken API ([PyPI allin1](https://pypi.org/pypi/allin1/json); [madmom #559](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/pull/559)).

**Main risks.** First, EDM label quality: pop-trained labels fail on EDM, so the rule layer carries the label quality until EDM weights exist, and its accuracy is unknown until the rating site measures it. Second, unreleased weights: allin1 v2 (Raveform) is "soon", EDMFormer states no release, and the fallback of fine-tuning on Raveform depends on re-downloading YouTube audio of unknown survival rate, under a dataset licence the project page does not state ([Raveform project page](https://mir-aidj.github.io/raveform/)). Third, licences: Essentia is AGPL and its models CC BY-NC-SA, MERT and MAEST are non-commercial, community RoFormer checkpoints vary and were not checked one by one, and Music Flamingo's terms are unverified. Running all of it as a separate Python process that only emits JSON keeps it out of the QLC+ binary, but this is not legal advice and matters only if the outputs or models ship. Fourth, runtime and MPS: there is no M1 Max measurement for any tool, MPS support is undocumented for Beat This, and RoFormer speed on Apple is known only relatively. Fifth, timing: MP3 decoder offsets of 20 to 40 ms and tempo changes destabilise grids, which Raveform's annotators also hit with rekordbox grids. Sixth, evaluation bias: anchoring on pre-filled output and tuning thresholds on the test set would inflate scores.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "can a model hear a drop" to "who supplies the EDM vocabulary". The signal for drops, buildups and breakdowns sits in a handful of curves (kick, bass and brightness on a downbeat grid), and stems from modern separators make those curves clean. What is missing is labelled EDM data in the model, and 2026 brought exactly that through Raveform, so the right design treats the section labeller as a swappable stage. Build the grid, stems, curves, JSON contract and rating loop now with a transparent rule layer, then replace the labeller when EDM weights appear, without touching QLC+.

The rating website is the most durable part of the plan, not an add-on. With no public EDM ground truth for vocals, instruments or mood, and only one public EDM structure benchmark, the user's own frozen 50 to 100 track set, plus title matches against Raveform and EDM-CUE and the existing VDJ grids, becomes the only honest measure of whether an analysis change improves the light show. Every later decision, including whether a synth-role probe or an audio LLM earns its runtime, should go through that loop first.
