# Measure structure against grids you already own

The beat grid is no longer the problem: on the 84-track test set the current pipeline (Beat This! `final0` plus a constant-tempo grid fit) matches VirtualDJ tempo within 0.05 BPM on **79/84 tracks**, puts bar 1 on VirtualDJ's anchor bar on **79/84**, and lands a consistent **+11 ms** after VirtualDJ's beats (p10 3 ms, p90 19 ms), well inside the standard ±70 ms beat window ([mir_eval beat](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/beat.html)). The "beats sound off" complaint most likely came from browser-side click scheduling, which is now fixed by mixing clicks server-side. What remains genuinely unproven is everything above the bar: phrases, section boundaries, labels and drops. For those, the only peer-reviewed EDM-trained model (Raveform All-In-One: beat F1 0.991, downbeat 0.965, boundary HR.5F 0.835) has **no released weights** ([Raveform, TISMIR](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288); [project page](https://mir-aidj.github.io/raveform/)), and the only runnable EDM-label model (EDMFormer) rests on a 10-track test set ([EDMFormer](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08759v1)). The defensible restart is therefore: keep the Beat This! grid, rebuild the structure layer as a transparent, peer-reviewed rule method (downbeat-snapped novelty on an 8-bar grid, labels from low-band and RMS energy), run EDMFormer as a challenger, and score every version against a fixed protocol that uses VirtualDJ and Mixed In Key markers as silver references for grid and phrase, and the user's own corrected annotations as the only gold for labels and drops.

Sources: peer-reviewed papers, official vendor documentation, and local measurements. "[local]" marks numbers from a read-only parse of the user's VirtualDJ `database.xml` (1,885 songs under ZZ_INCOMING) or from the coordinator's 84-track run; these have no external URL.

## Six labels reduce to low-band energy steps on an 8-bar grid

Musicology treats breakdown, build-up and drop as one unit, the "break routine": a sudden thinning, a layer-by-layer rebuild, and the moment bass and kick return, which produces "a characteristic U-shaped amplitude profile" ([Solberg & Dibben 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/145911/)). EDM tracks normally contain **two or more** break routines, with the later one meant to be most intense ([Solberg & Dibben 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/145911/); [Solberg 2014](https://dj.dancecult.net/index.php/dancecult/article/view/451)). Every label below can be stated as a measurable cue anchored to the downbeat grid, which is what makes a rule layer defensible at all.

| Label | Operational definition | Measurable cues | Grid rule | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beat | Constant period from a tracker, fitted linearly | Inter-beat interval; VDJ stores the period in seconds as Scan `Bpm` | Linear fit over the whole track (VDJ has no fluid grids in this DB [local]) | [VDJ database](https://virtualdj.com/wiki/VDJ_database.html) |
| Bar 1 / downbeat | First beat of a 4-beat bar | Kick and low-band accent, section changes landing on it | 1 bar = 4 beats from the anchor; VDJ's "shift downbeat" moves the anchor | [VDJScript verbs](https://virtualdj.com/manuals/virtualdj/appendix/vdjscriptverbs.html); [VDJ BPM editor](https://virtualdj.com/manuals/virtualdj/editors/bpmeditor.html) |
| Phrase | 8 bars (32 beats) as the unit, 16 bars as the modal section length | Layer entries and exits after 2, 4, 8, 16 or 32 bars | Soft prior, not a law: 4 of 160 D&B songs violated the multiple-of-8 rule | [Solberg 2014](https://dj.dancecult.net/index.php/dancecult/article/view/451); [Kim 2020](https://program.ismir2020.net/static/final_papers/352.pdf); [Vande Veire 2018](https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13636-018-0134-8) |
| Intro / outro | Percussive mixing zones with little harmonic content; "ambient" variants lack percussion | Kick present, low harmonic novelty, RMS below track mean | Starts at bar 1 / ends at track end, usually 16 or 32 bars (no published count) | [Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288) |
| Breakdown | Sharp energy drop; bass and usually kick removed | Step fall in sub-100 Hz energy and RMS | Starts on a phrase downbeat | [Solberg 2014](https://dj.dancecult.net/index.php/dancecult/article/view/451); [Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288) |
| Buildup | Monotonic rise in energy and tension | Rising brightness, risers (often white noise), filter opening, snare subdivision accelerating over the last ~4 bars | Ends on the drop downbeat; start is the least reliable boundary | [Smith 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A0748DBC5425594BE0F95D9AE0676DDC/core-reader); [Solberg 2014](https://dj.dancecult.net/index.php/dancecult/article/view/451) |
| Drop (event) | "where the buildup ends and the bassline is re-introduced" | Step return of kick and bass, often after a bar of low-band gap | One timestamp, on a downbeat | [Yadati 2014](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000297.pdf); [Solberg & Dibben 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/145911/) |
| Drop section (core) | Most energetic, full-texture part | RMS and spectral flux above track mean | Starts at a drop event | [Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288); [Vande Veire 2018](https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13636-018-0134-8) |
| Cooldown / bridge | Inverse of buildup; bridge is rare, mid-track, once | Gradual energy fall without percussion dominance | Phrase-aligned | [Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288) |
| Energy | "a combination of loudness, rhythmic density, frequency density, and instrumentation" | Per-bar loudness, onset density, band energies, stem activity | Per bar, smoothed over 1-2 bars | [Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288) |

Three definitional conflicts must be decided before any measuring starts, because they change the scores. First, **"drop" is a point** for the musicologists and Yadati but **a section** in Raveform, so the pipeline should emit both a drop event and a drop section. Second, the kick is not always absent in breakdowns: in festival house "the presence of drums in house breakdowns is more variable" ([Smith 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A0748DBC5425594BE0F95D9AE0676DDC/core-reader)), so kick absence alone is a poor breakdown detector and has to be combined with bass-band and overall RMS. Third, experts themselves collapse breakdown and buildup when "the breakdown leads fluidly into a buildup, so that only one formal section is perceived" ([Smith 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A0748DBC5425594BE0F95D9AE0676DDC/core-reader)); the protocol should accept a merged "breakdown-buildup" as correct rather than penalise it.

Two cues deserve special weight. Fills, risers and silences sit in the last 1 to 4 bars **before** a section downbeat, and Vande Veire found such "breaks" create false novelty peaks one or two measures before the true boundary ([Vande Veire 2018](https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13636-018-0134-8)). This matches the lesson already logged in this project that fill bars moved kick changes one bar early [local]. Anacrusis features should therefore predict the next downbeat, not mark a boundary themselves. No peer-reviewed source publishes numeric thresholds (dB drops, band edges, riser slopes) for any label, so every constant in the rule layer must be fitted on a dev split and never on the test set.

The Raveform table is the anchor for every expectation. The same architecture drops from **HR.5F 0.835 to 0.635** and from **PWF 0.847 to 0.542** when trained on pop instead of EDM ([Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)), so pop-trained labels are not a baseline worth running except as boundary proposers. The downbeat AMLt scores (0.792 for madmom, 0.812 for pop-trained All-In-One) sit well above their downbeat F1, which says generic downbeat errors are mostly metrical-phase errors, exactly what a track-wide phase vote fixes. The local 79/84 bar-1 agreement is consistent with that reading. An undocumented `raveform-fold3` checkpoint from October 2023 sits on Hugging Face but the loader cannot load it by name and it predates the paper by 20 months ([HF taejunkim/allinone](https://huggingface.co/taejunkim/allinone)); it is an experiment, not a baseline.

Two consequences follow. The strongest runnable, peer-reviewed structure method for EDM is not a neural model at all but Vande Veire's pipeline: MFCC and RMS self-similarity, checkerboard novelty, peaks snapped to the nearest downbeat and discarded beyond 0.4 bar, boundaries constrained to multiples of 8 bars, and segments labelled "high" when mean RMS exceeds the track mean ([Vande Veire 2018](https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13636-018-0134-8)). Its evidence is D&B only, so its numbers are a plausibility check for house and techno, not a promise. And EDMFormer, the only runnable EDM-label model, has a 10-track test set and a non-commercial dependency, so it belongs in the protocol as a challenger to be measured locally, not as the default.

## VirtualDJ and Mixed In Key markers are grid references, not structure truth

None of the user's DJ-software markers has a published accuracy figure, and the one peer-reviewed comparison is unflattering: against professional DJ cues, Mixed In Key 10 reaches **F1 0.22** at a one-beat tolerance, against 0.39 for CUE-DETR ([CUE-DETR](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06823v1)). Locally, though, the markers agree with each other far above chance, which makes them useful silver references for grid and phrase questions.

| Marker | What the vendor documents | Local statistics [local] | Worth as reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| VDJ Scan `Bpm` + beatgrid anchor | `Bpm` = seconds between beats; anchor = "first detected beat"; bars of 4 beats ([VDJ database](https://virtualdj.com/wiki/VDJ_database.html); [BPM editor](https://virtualdj.com/manuals/virtualdj/editors/bpmeditor.html)) | All 1,884 scanned songs from engine `Version="801"`; 1,796 have one anchor, none more than one; `Phase` equals the anchor in 1,795/1,796 | Silver for tempo and beats; probable downbeat |
| VDJ anchor as downbeat | Not stated by vendor | Anchor phase agrees with MIK Energy cues in 185/194 songs (95.4%) and VDJ End Break in 96/104 (92.3%) | Anchor-not-downbeat rate bounded at about 5 to 8% |
| VDJ "Break N" | Auto-created where "there is a definitive change in the audio" ([POI editor](https://virtualdj.com/manuals/virtualdj/editors/poieditor.html)) | 1,406/1,885 songs; 78.6% within 50 ms of a beat; dominant position **one beat before** a bar start (index 3 in 60/85 songs) | Weak breakdown-start candidate after snapping +1 beat |
| VDJ "End Break N" | Same, algorithm undocumented | 88.6% within 50 ms; bar start 84.0% (chance 25%), 8-bar line 48.9% (chance 3.1%); Break to End Break most often 33, 65, 97 beats | Section-start candidate; plausibly drop or return of full texture |
| MIK "Energy N" cues | "up to 8 automatic Cue Points" at "the most important parts" ([MIK](https://mixedinkey.com/learn-more/)); "intros, breakdowns, drops, and mix-out sections" ([MIK + Serato](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/use-mixed-in-key-with-serato/)) | 208 songs; raw offset to nearest beat **+49.1 ms** median (MP3 +49.2 ms, n=1,551; AIFF -2.1 ms, n=51); after removing it: 92.9% within 50 ms, bar start 94.9%, 4-bar 84.6%, 8-bar 75.2%, 16-bar 48.8%; gaps a multiple of 16 beats in 90.6% | Best local phrase-boundary silver; per-segment energy as ordinal reference |
| MIK "Cue N" cues | Same source, older batches | 925 songs; no offset (median -1.3 ms); bar start 77.3%, 8-bar 49.8% | Weaker phrase silver |
| MIK comment "10A - 7" | Energy Level 1-10, "how intense a song feels"; "a single track-level score cannot describe every breakdown" ([MIK energy](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/use-energy-level-detection/)) | 1,615 songs; 72% score 6 or 7; track energy within 1 of rounded mean cue energy in 86.1% (n=208) | Little spread at track level; use per-cue values instead |

The MIK offset is a decoding artefact, not a placement error: it appears on MP3s and not on AIFFs, and it varies by import batch (+50 ms in five folders, +24 ms in one) [local]. Any millisecond-level comparison has to subtract it per file or per batch, while beat-relative tolerances (half a beat is about 238 ms at 126 BPM) are unaffected. The same mechanism explains why both allin1 and Raveform warn that MP3 decoders shift timing by 20 to 40 ms ([allin1](https://github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one); [Raveform project page](https://mir-aidj.github.io/raveform/)). The pipeline's own +11 ms against VDJ is in the same family: constant across tracks, small, and below the 40 ms tick of QLC+'s 25 Hz MasterTimer [local], so it should be stored as one calibration constant and applied only when a show follows VDJ's playback position, not chased as a tracking error.

The 5/84 bar-1 disagreements need listening, not more tuning. VDJ's anchor itself disagrees with two independent phase references in 5 to 8% of songs [local], so a 6% disagreement rate between the pipeline and VDJ is as likely to be VDJ's error as the pipeline's. The same holds for the 5 tempo mismatches; constant-tempo grids from both tools will fail on tracks with real tempo changes, which Raveform's annotators also hit with rekordbox grids ([Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)). MIK's low F1 against DJ cues comes mostly from recall (0.25 at 1 beat): MIK picks a different subset of boundaries, not off-grid ones ([CUE-DETR](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06823v1)). That dictates how to use it: check whether the pipeline's boundaries **cover** MIK and End Break markers (recall against silver), and never penalise the pipeline for boundaries MIK did not mark.

## Baseline stack: keep the grid, rebuild sections as a cited rule layer

The recommended baseline changes only the structure layer and adds measurement. The `.vdjstems` files already present for 817 of 827 MP3s carry separate kick and bass streams [local], which deliver the single strongest drop and breakdown cue (low-band return and removal) without running a separator.

| Stage | Choice | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| Decode | ffmpeg once to PCM; one decoder for everything; store the measured offset to VDJ (+11 ms) as a constant | MP3 decoder offsets ([allin1](https://github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one)); local offsets |
| Beats | Keep Beat This! `final0` + linear constant-tempo fit; flag tracks where the fit residual or VDJ tempo disagrees | 79/84 tempo parity [local]; MIT ([repo](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this)) |
| Bar 1 | Beat This! downbeats + track-wide phase vote preferring the dominant phase; auto-flag when it disagrees with the VDJ anchor or MIK cue phase | AMLt vs F1 gap ([Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)); 79/84 [local] |
| Phrase grid | 8-bar grid from bar 1, 16-bar modal, as a soft prior; allow off-grid boundaries only on strong novelty | [Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288); [Vande Veire 2018](https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13636-018-0134-8) |
| Boundaries | MFCC + RMS self-similarity novelty on mix and stems, snap to downbeat, drop peaks beyond 0.4 bar, prefer 8-bar multiples, suppress peaks 1-2 bars before a stronger one | [Vande Veire 2018](https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13636-018-0134-8) |
| Labels | Per phrase: high/low by mean RMS vs track mean; breakdown = low-band (kick + bass stem) step fall; buildup = low phase with rising brightness and onset density; drop event = first downbeat where kick and bass return after a low phase; intro/outro = edge phrases | [Vande Veire 2018](https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13636-018-0134-8); [Solberg & Dibben 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/145911/); [Yadati 2014](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000297.pdf) |
| Energy | Per-bar z-scored composite of loudness, low-band energy, onset density and brightness, smoothed over 1-2 bars | Raveform's energy definition ([Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)) |
| Challenger | EDMFormer on the same test set; swap in Raveform All-In-One when v2 ships | [EDMFormer](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08759v1); [project page](https://mir-aidj.github.io/raveform/) |

### Evaluation protocol: silver for the grid, gold for the music

Every system version is run over the frozen 84-track set and scored per task against the reference that task can bear. Tolerances follow the grid, because a lighting cue snapped to a downbeat is either on the right bar or not; seconds-wide windows like Yadati's 3 to 15 s hide that distinction.

| Task | Reference | Metric | Tolerance | Proposed target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tempo | VDJ Scan `Bpm` (silver) | Share within 0.05 BPM; octave errors counted separately | 0.05 BPM | Hold at least 79/84; every miss listened to |
| Beats | VDJ grid after removing the global offset (silver) | `mir_eval.beat.f_measure`, CMLt, AMLt | ±70 ms, CMLt ±17.5% of period | F1 at least 0.95 (generic trackers score 0.930 to 0.947 on EDM) |
| Bar 1 | VDJ anchor phase, cross-checked by MIK cue phase (silver); user verdict on every disagreement (gold) | Phase agreement mod 4 beats | Exact bar | At least 94% (VDJ's own agreement with MIK is 95.4%) |
| Phrase grid | MIK Energy cues (offset removed) and VDJ End Break (silver) | Share of silver markers on the pipeline's 8-bar lines | 1 beat | At least the 75% MIK itself reaches against VDJ |
| Boundaries | User-corrected annotations (gold); MIK + End Break for recall only (silver) | `mir_eval.segment.detection` HR.5F and HR3F, plus hit rate at 1 beat and 1 bar | 0.5 s, 3 s, 1 beat, 1 bar | HR3F above 0.635 (pop-model level on EDM); 0.835 is the in-domain reference |
| Labels | User annotations only (gold), merged breakdown-buildup accepted | Pairwise F, Sf (NCE), frame accuracy for EDMFormer comparison, with a published label mapping | 0.1 s frames | Beat EDMFormer on the same tracks |
| Drops | User annotations (gold) | Event F1 at 1 beat, 1 bar, 4 bars; drop-count error; main-drop error in bars | Beat-relative | Main drop within 1 bar; no published target exists |
| Energy | MIK per-cue energy (ordinal silver, small n) | Within-track rank correlation of segment energy with MIK cue energy | Per segment | No published target; report only |

The metric choices come from the field's own conventions: Raveform reports beat and downbeat F1 at ±70 ms with CMLt and AMLt, and HR.5F, PWF and Sf for sections ([Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)); CUE-DETR scores at one beat and half a beat ([CUE-DETR](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06823v1)); and Nieto et al. recommend hit rates over median deviation and note that listeners prefer fewer correct boundaries to more imprecise ones ([Nieto et al. 2020](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.54)). `mir_eval` trims beats before 5 s by default ([mir_eval beat](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/beat.html)); bar 1 is scored separately so intros are not lost to the trim. The proposed targets are this report's judgement, set from published comparison points, not published thresholds.

The user's ratings are the gold layer, and the rating categories should map one-to-one onto these metrics: "correct", "off by N bars early/late", "wrong label", "not a boundary" and "missed here" yield hit rates at every bar tolerance, label confusion and recall without further annotation. Pre-filled output biases annotators toward the model, so 10 to 20 tracks should be annotated from scratch, twice and weeks apart, to give a personal agreement ceiling; no EDM dataset publishes one. For calibration, professional DJs placing cues in the same track agree exactly only 23.6% of the time, within 1 bar 40.4% and within 8 bars 73.6% ([Kim 2020](https://program.ismir2020.net/static/final_papers/352.pdf)), so boundary disagreement beyond drops is partly irreducible. Thresholds are tuned on a separate dev split, system versions are compared per track with a paired Wilcoxon test, and a per-track 1 to 5 "usable for lighting" score serves as the product metric against which the objective metrics are checked. An 84-track set is in line with the published EDM test sets of 98 and 101 tracks ([EDMFormer](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08759v1); [CUE-DETR](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06823v1)).

## Risks: labels stay unmeasured until the user annotates

The largest risk is that section labels and drops have no reference except the user. VDJ and MIK markers say where something changes, never what it is, and drop versus breakdown can only be inferred from MIK's energy change between consecutive cues, which MIK does not define officially ([MIK energy](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/use-energy-level-detection/)). Coverage is also thin where it matters: only 208 of 1,885 songs carry MIK Energy cues [local], so the 84-track set probably holds only a handful with the best phrase silver; that count should be taken before relying on the phrase and energy rows.

Several gaps limit what the baseline can claim. No peer-reviewed numeric thresholds exist for any label, so the rule layer's constants are fitted, not cited. The musicology behind the break routine is mostly house at 118 to 128 BPM ([Solberg & Dibben 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/145911/)), Vande Veire's segmentation numbers are D&B only, techno's gradual fades are intrinsically ambiguous even for Raveform's experts ([Raveform](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)), and no peer-reviewed source covers hardstyle or dubstep drop conventions. Harmonix's 129 dance tracks contain 4 "build" and 0 "drop" labels ([Harmonix segments](https://github.com/urinieto/harmonixset/tree/main/dataset/segments)), so no public pop dataset can stand in. The only external EDM gold with audio-matched labels, Raveform's CC BY 4.0 annotations, requires matching owned files by title or retrieving YouTube audio of unknown survival rate.

Model availability and licences shape the upgrade path. Raveform's weights have no release date in any official source, its GitHub repo holds only a licence file, and allin1 on PyPI is still the 2023 release ([GitHub mir-aidj/raveform](https://github.com/mir-aidj/raveform); [PyPI allin1](https://pypi.org/project/allin1/)). EDMFormer and SongFormer inherit MuQ's CC-BY-NC-4.0 licence ([MuQ](https://huggingface.co/OpenMuQ/MuQ-large-msd-iter)), which matters only if models or outputs ship. Finally, the stems carry their own timing and integrity risk: one `.vdjstems` file was truncated and had to fall back to demucs [local], and stem-to-mix alignment has not been measured, so a drop event derived from the kick stem should be checked against the mix onset before it is trusted at beat precision.

## Conclusion

The first version failed for two different reasons that the new protocol separates. Its grid was largely right but sounded wrong because of playback, and its sections were unmeasured rather than demonstrably wrong. Treating VDJ and MIK as silver references turns two questions (tempo and bar 1) into checks that can run on every build, and leaves the user's listening time for the questions only a human can answer: which phrase is the drop, and does the light show feel right.

The decisive asymmetry is that structure evidence for EDM is strong in one unreleased model and thin everywhere else, while grid evidence is strong and local. That argues for freezing the grid now, investing in the gold annotation set rather than in rule tuning, and keeping the labeller swappable: when Raveform's weights appear, the same 84 tracks and the same table will say within an hour whether it beats the rule layer on this library, which no paper can.
